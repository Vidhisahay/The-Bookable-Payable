# Design notes

## What I learned about the documents

A PDF is an evidence bundle, not necessarily one invoice. It can contain a payable, continuation pages, remittance information, or supporting documents. Repeated invoice numbers and tax figures in attachments do not create additional obligations. The invoice gross, a cash amount after withholding, and a tax-purpose currency conversion are different values. The ERP total is a useful arithmetic check, but matching it does not prove the extracted line and tax structure is faithful.

## What the system does with unfamiliar evidence

`ingestion.pipeline.IngestionPipeline` extracts native PDF text when it is usable and otherwise applies local Tesseract OCR page by page with bounded retries. Successful OCR is cached atomically by PDF content, page, OCR settings, engine/model identity, and processing version. Timeout and empty results are not cached. The classifier uses document-text cues and keeps page-level evidence and uncertainty. `ingestion.payables.extractor.extract_payables` extracts candidate fields and retains page references and field statuses; it does not fill absent amounts to make totals balance. `ingestion.autodraft_pipeline.AutodraftPipeline` scopes invoice pages, resolves only supported master-data matches, maps candidates to the required keys, and keeps diagnostics outside the accounting JSON.

`ingestion.autodraft.reconcile` calls the supplied `erp_book` with the extracted components. The pipeline emits a payable only when the arithmetic passes, supplier identity is supported by a source-evidenced name or VAT ID, and schema enums are valid. A failed or incomplete case remains in diagnostics. Uncertain documents stay unresolved rather than being forced into `declined`. Matching is deterministic and text based; there is no trained model or paid service. The run guide documents the command and output folders.

The extractor now accepts unnumbered rows when a table header explicitly identifies description, quantity, unit price, tax, and amount columns. It keeps row tax rates at line scope and removes a matching header tax summary only when the printed line bases/rates reproduce the printed tax total. For party identity, explicit supplier labels and VAT-linked evidence take priority; conflicting legal entities in flattened OCR are left unresolved instead of choosing the first company name. The OCR text currently omits bounding boxes, so some visually distinct buyer/supplier columns still need spatial extraction or manual review.

For scanned pages whose OCR exposes an `Activity / Tax / Qty / Rate / Amount` table, ingestion keeps a separate, cached 220-DPI table-oriented OCR result. For this layout, line prices are marked net only when printed line extensions sum exactly to the printed subtotal and the printed header tax plus subtotal equals the printed gross. Itemized transport rows remain line items rather than being duplicated as header freight charges.

## A document that remains unresolved

`INV-31.pdf` is still unresolved. The native text contains `$17,657.53`, but `$` alone does not identify USD, CAD, SGD, or another dollar currency. The remittance and billing pages also contain date clues that do not establish one invoice date, and the current extractor does not recover supported electricity line components from the billing layout. Without those components and a currency, a schema-valid draft cannot be checked meaningfully against the ERP. I have left the payable un-emitted rather than choosing a currency or reconstructing lines from the gross.

`INV-14.pdf` and `INV-15.pdf` have source-supported invoice numbers, dates, amounts, and table rows, and their extracted tax rates remain on the individual lines. They are not emitted: the flattened party text interleaves buyer/supplier blocks, and the `Business Rates` and `Utilities` rows do not establish an allowed line item type. The parser leaves those decisions unresolved instead of assigning the company nearest a tax label or guessing an accounting type.

`INV-01.pdf` illustrates a different limit: the extracted dates, EUR 438 gross, two lines, and zero-rate reverse-charge context support an ERP arithmetic result of EUR 438. Its supplier legal name is not printed clearly enough to extract, but its printed VAT ID supports identifying the supplier identity field. Since the schema permits a blank supplier code and the supplied master data has no defensible matching record, the system emits the payable with the VAT ID and a blank supplier code. The ERP calculation is EUR 438.00.

## Current run and trade-offs

The latest full run used a 60-second OCR budget and one retry. It produced 42 accounting JSON files and 42 diagnostics files: four payables (INV-01, INV-27, INV-36, and credit memo DU-10), one confirmed decline (DU-08, a payment reminder), and 37 unresolved documents. There were no timed-out pages; INV-23 pages 2 and 3 are blank in the source and produced no OCR text. The other 109 pages yielded 99 OCR results and 10 native-text results. Of 33 extracted candidates, five passed arithmetic reconciliation and 28 remained unresolved. INV-02 passed arithmetic but was not emitted because its second line's item type was not established. INV-27 reconciles to KES 70,654.30: its seven printed activity amounts total KES 60,908.88, and the separate 16% VAT amount is KES 9,745.42. Its unlabelled reference and locale-ambiguous date remain blank. INV-01 reconciles to EUR 438.00; INV-36 to USD 91,580.50; and DU-10 to GBP 5,076.17. DU-10's product table supports two goods lines with 20% VAT shown on each line, while the header VAT is a summary; its supplier VAT ID is taken from the seller's footer, not the buyer's VAT block. Its supplier code remains blank because the master data has no defensible match. Cached pages were reused during the full run, including the targeted activity-table OCR result. Passing tests does not validate the remaining real-document outputs.

The standard dataset command is documented in `RUN_GUIDE.md`; diagnostics now record the run's OCR settings and per-attempt timing. Once a page is cached, reruns reuse the same successful text; failed, blank, and sparse results are retried only within a fixed budget. Further improvement should focus on source review and layout-aware line and item-type extraction for selected difficult pages. Validation remains strict so that a higher payable count does not come from guessed supplier names, currencies, line types, or tax treatments.
